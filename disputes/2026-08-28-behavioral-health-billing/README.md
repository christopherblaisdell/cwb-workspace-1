## Case theory

Two independent tracks, pursued in parallel. Track A is where the money is; Track B is
where the leverage is.

**Track A — The charge is not substantiated.**
The facility bills clinical rates. If the record shows 12-step mutual-help meetings
occupying hours billed as clinical group psychotherapy, or groups "facilitated" by
unlicensed staff, or a group the patient was excluded from but still billed for, then
the charges are for services not rendered. A copay is a percentage or per-unit share of
services actually delivered — reduce the services, reduce the copay. Separately, the
copay figure itself must be verified against the plan's out-of-pocket maximum and
against mental health parity requirements. See
[05](05-insurance-appeal.md) and [04](04-formal-billing-dispute.md).

**Track B — Denial of secular treatment and exclusion from a group on religious grounds.**
The facility offered only 12-step programming, provided no secular evidence-based
alternative, and excluded the patient from at least one treatment group because he is an
atheist. In Florida this runs through statutory patient rights (Fla. Stat. § 397.501 /
§ 394.459, right to individual dignity), licensure standards requiring individualized
treatment planning, accreditation standards, and FDUTPA (§ 501.201) for deceptive
marketing. See [08](08-religious-discrimination-and-secular-treatment.md) and
[09](09-florida-agencies-and-law.md).

These reinforce each other. Track B explains *why* the programming wasn't clinical,
which is exactly what Track A needs to prove — and the group incident is itself direct
evidence that the session billed as psychotherapy was not psychotherapy.

## How to make a request "official" and create a paper trail

Do **not** rely on a phone call. Use as many of the following simultaneously as you
can — redundancy is the point. Each one independently proves receipt.

### Tier 1 — Strongest (use at least two)

| Method | Why it works | Cost / notes |
|---|---|---|
| **USPS Certified Mail with Return Receipt (PS Form 3811, the green card)** | Produces a signed receipt from the facility proving *who* signed and *when*. This is the gold standard and is what a regulator or judge will expect to see. | ~$9. Keep the green card. Also request **Certificate of Mailing (PS Form 3817)** to prove the date you sent it. |
| **USPS Certified Mail + Electronic Return Receipt** | Same as above but delivery proof is emailed as a PDF and archived on usps.com. Easier to store digitally. | Slightly cheaper. Save the PDF to `evidence/`. |
| **Fax with transmission confirmation report** | Healthcare runs on fax. The confirmation page (with date, time, receiving fax number, page count, "OK" status) is contemporaneous proof of delivery. Providers cannot claim a fax was "lost in the mail." | Use an online fax service (HelloFax, eFax, FaxZero) — it emails you a PDF confirmation. Send to the **billing department fax** and the **medical records / HIM fax**. |

### Tier 2 — Supporting (use in addition, never instead)

| Method | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Patient portal secure message** | Lands inside *their own* system with *their own* timestamp. Screenshot the sent message and the timestamp. Very hard for them to disclaim. |
| **Email to billing, medical records, patient advocate, and the Administrator/CEO** | Cheap, instant, and CC'ing multiple recipients makes "we never got it" implausible. Request a read receipt. Keep the sent copy. |
| **Hand delivery** | Bring two copies. Have the front desk date-stamp/sign your copy as received. Photograph it immediately. |

### Tier 3 — Escalation levers (hold in reserve, then use)

- **HIPAA Right of Access.** Your request for your own records is a legal demand
  under **45 C.F.R. § 164.524**. The facility must act within **30 calendar days**
  (one 30-day extension allowed, but only with written notice to you). Missing that
  deadline is itself a federal violation.
- **HHS Office for Civil Rights (OCR) complaint.** If they blow the 30 days, file at
  `ocrportal.hhs.gov/ocr/cp`. OCR has fined providers specifically for records-access
  stonewalling. Citing this deadline in the letter itself often produces a response.
- **State medical records statute.** Most states have their own deadline (often
  10–30 days) with penalties. Add the citation for [YOUR STATE] to the letter.
- **State licensing agency + accreditor (Joint Commission / CARF).** Unresponsiveness
  to a patient grievance is independently reportable.

### Rules to follow every single time

1. **Never negotiate or dispute by phone.** If they finally call, say: "Please put
   that in writing and send it to my address on file." Then email them a summary of
   the call the same day ("As discussed today at 2:15 p.m., you stated ...") — an
   unrebutted written summary becomes evidence.
2. **Send to multiple addresses.** Billing, Medical Records/HIM, Patient Advocate,
   Compliance Officer, and the **registered agent** listed for the corporate entity
   on your Secretary of State's business search. The registered agent cannot ignore
   service and this frequently unsticks a stonewall.
3. **Always state a deadline** and what happens when it passes.
4. **Always dispute the debt in the same letter** so the balance is formally
   contested while records are pending.
5. **Log everything** in [communication-log.md](communication-log.md) the same day.
6. **Keep originals.** Never send originals of anything. Scan to `evidence/`.

## Where to find the addresses you need

- **Facility billing/medical records fax + address** — bottom of the bill, the
  facility website "Contact" or "Medical Records" page, or your admission paperwork.
- **Compliance Officer / Privacy Officer** — required to be named in the Notice of
  Privacy Practices you signed at admission. Address the letter to them by title if
  you don't have a name.
- **Registered agent & corporate parent** — your Secretary of State business entity
  search. Many behavioral health facilities are owned by a larger corporation; send
  a copy to corporate compliance too.
- **Accreditor** — Joint Commission "Quality Check" (`qualitycheck.org`) or CARF
  provider directory will tell you who accredits them.
