# 23 — Facility Conditions: No Bathroom Locks, Failed A/C, Child on Premises

Three further conditions, each independently reportable, and each compounding the others.

---

## 1. ⭐ Bathrooms had no locks

**Reported:** bathroom doors had no locks, and residents **frequently walked in on one
another.**

In any residential setting that's a dignity problem. In **this** facility it is
considerably worse, because of what else was happening in the building.

### Why this is serious here specifically

| Context | Consequence |
|---|---|
| **Two sexual assaults** were reported during your stay ([19](19-intoxicated-resident-assault.md)) | Unlockable bathrooms in a facility with a demonstrated assault problem is a foreseeable-risk failure |
| **Mixed-gender population** | Residents undressing with no ability to secure the door |
| **Staff told you to change in the bathroom** to avoid the bedroom cameras ([15](15-surveillance-cameras.md)) | The facility's own answer to a privacy complaint was a room offering no privacy |
| **Residents in withdrawal** | Frequent, urgent, and prolonged bathroom use, often while physically unwell |

That third row is the one to lead with. A resident raised a privacy concern about cameras
recording him undressing, and a nurse directed him to undress in a room **that could not be
closed.** The facility had no private space at all — not the bedrooms, not the bathrooms.

### Reportable to

- **DCF / AHCA** — patient dignity and privacy under Fla. Stat. § 397.501 / § 394.459;
  licensure standards under Fla. Admin. Code r. 65D-30
- **The Joint Commission** — privacy and dignity standards
- Relevant to any **failure-to-protect** analysis regarding the assaults

### Questions

- Were locks missing from **all** bathrooms, or some?
- Were they removed, broken, or never installed? *(Some facilities intentionally remove
  bathroom locks for suicide-prevention reasons — a legitimate practice. **But that
  requires compensating measures**: privacy signage, occupancy indicators, staff
  monitoring, or single-occupancy scheduling. Were any in place?)*
- Shared or single-occupancy bathrooms?
- Gender-separated?
- Did you or anyone else report it? Response?
- Did anyone walk in on you, or you on someone else?

> ⚠️ **Be fair on this point.** If the locks were removed for safety reasons, say so — and
> then make the real argument: that the facility removed the locks **and** provided no
> alternative privacy measure **and** told a patient to use that room to escape a camera.
> The honest version is the stronger one.

## 2. ⭐ Air conditioning not working properly

**Reported:** the A/C did not work properly and it was **very hot** — hot enough that you
had to sleep in underwear only.

### Why this is more than discomfort

**This is Florida.** Depending on the season, an inadequately cooled residential facility is
a genuine health issue — and for **this** population, more so:

- **Withdrawal causes temperature dysregulation**, sweating, and fluid loss. Heat compounds
  dehydration in patients already at risk.
- Many psychiatric medications — **antipsychotics, anticholinergics, some antidepressants**
  — impair thermoregulation and raise heat-illness risk. Patients on these drugs are a
  recognized vulnerable group in heat.
- Detox patients may be sedated, less able to recognize or respond to overheating.

**And it directly caused your exposure on camera.** You didn't choose to sleep undressed.
The building was too hot to do otherwise. The facility's failure to maintain the A/C created
the condition that its own unlawful camera system then recorded and displayed to staff and
every patient at the medication window.

**State that causal chain explicitly** in the DCF, AHCA, and Joint Commission complaints. It
is the difference between two separate gripes and one coherent failure.

### Reportable to

- **DCF / AHCA** — physical plant and environmental standards for licensed facilities
- **The Joint Commission** — environment of care standards
- **Martin County Building Department** — habitability

### Preserve

Add to the demands in [15](15-surveillance-cameras.md):

- HVAC maintenance, repair, and service records for your dates of service
- Work orders, vendor invoices, service tickets
- Any temperature logs for resident areas
- Any complaints from residents or staff about heat

### Questions

- Whole building, or your room / one wing?
- How hot, roughly? Any thermometer visible?
- Did you report it? To whom? Response?
- Did other residents complain?
- Was it ever repaired during your stay?
- Were fans or any other relief provided?
- What time of year was your admission?

## 3. ⚠️ A counselor brought their child onto the premises

**Reported:** a counselor brought their child to the facility. You don't believe the child
went behind the medication counter.

### Why this matters

**Florida requires background screening** for personnel and others with access to
vulnerable patients in licensed facilities (Fla. Stat. ch. 435, level 2 screening).
*Verify application to this license type.* An unscreened individual on a residential unit
with adults in acute withdrawal is a licensure problem regardless of who they are.

Beyond screening:

- **Patient confidentiality** — an unauthorized person on the unit sees who is in
  treatment. Under **42 C.F.R. Part 2**, that fact is protected for every resident present.
- **Child safety** — a minor on a residential detox unit where, during the same period, a
  resident was intoxicated to the point of collapse and two sexual assaults were reported.
- **Staffing** — a counselor who brought a child to work may not have had childcare, which
  raises questions about scheduling, coverage, and whether the facility was adequately
  staffed. That connects to the census pressure already documented in
  [17](17-witnessed-patient-fall.md).

### Handle this one carefully

- **Report it factually and without embellishment.** You saw a staff member's child on the
  premises. That's it.
- **Do not identify or describe the child.**
- **Do not suggest anything happened to the child.** You haven't said that and shouldn't
  imply it.
- The reportable issue is the **facility's** failure to control who is on a residential
  unit — not the counselor's parenting.

### Questions

- How often — once, or regularly?
- Approximate age of the child?
- How long were they there?
- Where in the facility — office areas, or resident living space?
- Did the child interact with residents?
- Did other staff or residents remark on it?
