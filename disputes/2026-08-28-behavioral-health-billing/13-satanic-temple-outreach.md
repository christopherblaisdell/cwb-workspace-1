## Why membership materially strengthens the case

Up to now this file has framed you as an atheist — someone with **no** religion. That
framing works for dignity and individualized-treatment arguments, but it's weaker under
frameworks that protect *religion* as a class, because it invites the (wrong but common)
response that "you don't have a religion to discriminate against."

**As a member of The Satanic Temple, you are an adherent of a religion the IRS
recognized as a church in 2019.** That reframes the claim:

| Framing | Claim |
|---|---|
| Old | "I have no religious beliefs and was excluded." |
| New | **"I am a member of a recognized religion. The facility required participation in the religious practice of a different faith, refused to accommodate mine, and excluded me from treatment when I disclosed it."** |

The second is a straightforward religious discrimination claim, and it slots directly
into:

- **Florida patient rights** — nondiscriminatory service delivery (Fla. Stat. § 397.501)
- **Joint Commission standards** — respect for patients' cultural and **spiritual** values
- **FDUTPA** — the failure to disclose that programming was religious in nature
- **Any accommodation analysis** — you were asking for accommodation of a religious
  identity, not merely opting out

And the comparison is now precise and ugly for them: their own website celebrates that a
Christian patient **got his own Bible study group**. See
[11](11-facility-profile-remedy-therapy.md). A member of one recognized religion was
accommodated enthusiastically enough that they published it as marketing. A member of
another was polled about his beliefs and then had a staff member turn his back on him.

### One caution

**Do not let this become about theology.** Some regulators and adjudicators will react to
"Satanic Temple" with reflex rather than analysis. The framing that works is neutral and
procedural:

> "I am a member of a religious organization recognized as a church by the IRS. The
> facility's programming required participation in the religious practice of a different
> tradition. I requested a secular clinical alternative — cognitive behavioral therapy,
> which the facility advertises. I was refused, and I was subsequently excluded from a
> treatment group after disclosing my beliefs."

Every clause there is factual, verifiable, and hard to argue with. Nothing invites a
fight about what you believe.

Use TST membership where it establishes protected status. Don't lead with it in the
billing correspondence — letters #01 through #04 should stay focused on documentation
and charges.

## Who to contact at TST

**Verify current contact routes at `thesatanictemple.com` — structures and staff change.**

### 1. TST Legal / general legal intake

TST has an active legal operation and has litigated religious liberty and
religious-discrimination matters, particularly involving unequal treatment of
non-Christian and nontheistic adherents. Use the contact form on their site and ask to
be routed to legal intake.

Realistic expectations: they receive many requests and take on matters that fit their
strategic priorities. A billing dispute alone likely won't. **Your case has features that
might** — a religious-accommodation denial in a healthcare setting, documented disparate
treatment evidenced by the facility's own marketing, and a licensed, accredited provider.

### 2. ⭐ Grey Faction — the best fit

**Grey Faction is TST's campaign focused specifically on accountability in the mental
health industry.** Its entire remit is misconduct by mental health professionals and
treatment providers — exactly the domain your complaint sits in.

Of everything at TST, this is the most relevant place to send your account. Look for
Grey Faction's contact route through `thesatanictemple.com` or `greyfaction.org`.

### 3. Your local congregation / chapter

Chapters sometimes have members with relevant professional backgrounds and can escalate
internally. Faster than national in many cases.

## What to send them

Keep it short. One page, with attachments. Nobody reads a ten-page first contact.

```
Subject: Religious discrimination in a Florida residential treatment facility —
         TST member — request for guidance

I am a member of The Satanic Temple, writing about religious discrimination I
experienced as a patient at Remedy Therapy Behavioral Health, a Joint
Commission-accredited residential treatment facility in Stuart, Florida.

Summary:

1.  I admitted voluntarily while in crisis. Programming was almost entirely
    twelve-step based, which is religious in content.

2.  I requested a secular, evidence-based alternative — cognitive behavioral
    therapy. None was provided. The facility's own website advertises that it
    "utilize[s] a range of treatment options, including cognitive-behavioral
    therapy."

3.  A group facilitator polled participants on their religious beliefs. When I
    stated my beliefs, he began a prayer with the other participants, put his
    arms around them, turned his back to me, and said "SOME of us will be
    praying."

4.  The facility publishes a patient testimonial on its own website in which a
    Christian patient states he "was able to have MY OWN BIBLE STUDY GROUP." A
    member of one religion was accommodated and it was used as marketing. My
    request was refused.

5.  I am being billed $4,000 in copay. The facility has not responded to written
    requests for an itemized bill or my medical records. I also did not sign the
    intake paperwork — facility staff signed my name.

I am pursuing complaints with Florida DCF, the Joint Commission, and the Florida
Attorney General, and a written dispute with the facility. I would appreciate any
guidance, referral, or interest your legal team or Grey Faction may have.

Documentation available: full correspondence file, archived website captures,
signed declaration regarding the group incident, billing records.

Christopher Wayne Blaisdell
1936 Chathamoor Drive, Orlando, FL 32835
[PHONE] | [EMAIL]
```

**Attach:** your signed incident statement, the archived website captures (especially the
CBT paragraph and the Bible-study testimonial), and the facility profile.

| **Americans United for Separation of Church and State** | Litigates religious coercion, including in publicly funded programs. Stronger fit if any government funding is involved. | `au.org` |
| **Disability Rights Florida** | Florida's federally funded P&A agency for behavioral health. **Free, local, and obligated to help.** Start here — likely fastest response. | 1-800-342-0823 |

> **Realistic note:** advocacy organizations receive far more requests than they can take.
> Contact several, keep each message short and factual, and continue the regulatory and
> billing tracks regardless. Those are within your control. Outside help is upside.
