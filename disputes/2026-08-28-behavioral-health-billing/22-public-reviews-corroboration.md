This is a **public, dated, named, independent account** — written before you contacted
anyone, with no connection to your case. It corroborates four separate things you've told
me:

| Your account | Her review |
|---|---|
| Devices confiscated and locked up | "refused to give me my phone, or any possessions" |
| Blanket policy | "the rules change for each patient regarding electronics" |
| Misrepresentation of the program | "This place lies all the time" |
| Length-of-stay concerns ([19](19-intoxicated-resident-assault.md)) | Told 4 weeks, actually a **3 month** program |

### Why each piece matters

**1. Property withheld after discharge.** She left AMA and they refused to return her
phone and possessions, telling her to come back in two weeks. That goes beyond restricting
device use during treatment — it's withholding personal property from someone who has
left. Raises conversion, and it's a licensure issue.

**2. "The rules change for each patient."** This cuts against the facility's best defense.
If restrictions were clinically individualized, inconsistency would be expected and
explainable. But her framing is that the rules were applied *arbitrarily* — which is
neither a blanket clinical policy nor genuine individualization. **Either way they lose:**
blanket means not individualized; arbitrary means not clinical.

**3. ⭐ Length of stay — this corroborates the most serious billing question.** She was
told four weeks, then told it was a three-month program. Read that against the report in
[19](19-intoxicated-resident-assault.md) that a female patient's stay was **extended after
she was assaulted**, and against the fact that the facility placed a sedated detox patient
upstairs **because it was over census**.

Three independent data points all pointing the same direction: **this facility has a
pattern of keeping beds filled longer than patients expected or agreed to.** Longer stays
are more billable days. That is a question for regulators and for your lawyer.

**4. "This place lies all the time."** Straight to FDUTPA. Combined with the website
advertising CBT that you were denied and "delicious catered meals" from a 67°F
refrigerator.

## The rating distribution is worth noting

From the Birdeye aggregation of Google reviews (160 total, 4.4 average):

| Stars | Count |
|---|---|
| ★★★★★ | 129 |
| ★★★★ | 7 |
| ★★★ | 0 |
| ★★ | 3 |
| ★ | **21** |

**21 one-star reviews and zero three-star reviews.** A sharply bimodal distribution with an
empty middle is a pattern worth flagging to counsel. It doesn't prove anything on its own —
but a high average driven by many five-star reviews alongside a substantial block of
one-star reviews is the shape you'd expect if favourable reviews were being solicited while
serious complaints accumulated separately.

**Note also:** the facility uses **Birdeye**, a reputation-management platform. That is
entirely legal and extremely common. But it means the review profile is actively managed,
and your lawyer may want to know how five-star reviews were obtained — particularly whether
current patients were asked to write them while still in treatment and dependent on staff.

## ⚠️ Do not respond to or post reviews

Do not write a review of the facility, do not reply to any existing review, and do not
contact any reviewer.

- A public post can be used to argue you're pursuing a grudge rather than a claim
- It gives the facility an opportunity to respond publicly with your health information
- Contacting reviewers looks like witness solicitation

**Your lawyer or a regulator can approach them properly. You should not.**

Read them. Screenshot them. Say nothing.
