# 05 — Insurance Appeal

> **Send by:** Certified Mail w/ Return Receipt to the appeals address on your EOB,
> plus upload through the member portal if available.
>
> **DEADLINE — ACT NOW:** Internal appeals are typically due **180 days from the date
> on the EOB**. This clock runs independently of the facility's stonewalling. Do not
> wait for the facility's records to file. File a placeholder appeal preserving your
> rights and supplement it later.

---

## First: figure out what kind of $4,000 this is

Pull the EOB for every claim in the episode and check:

- [ ] **Claim paid, you owe cost-sharing.** The $4,000 is deductible/coinsurance/copay.
      → Your fight is mostly with the *facility* (letters #01/#04), not the insurer.
      Still verify the facility didn't balance-bill you above the allowed amount.
- [ ] **Claim denied or days cut** for "not medically necessary" / "level of care not
      justified" / "failed concurrent review."
      → Appeal to the insurer. This letter. Strong odds — behavioral health level-of-care
      denials are overturned frequently on external review.
- [ ] **Out-of-network balance billing.** Facility billed you the difference between
      charge and allowed amount.
      → Check the **No Surprises Act**. For emergency care and certain facility
      situations, balance billing is prohibited outright.
- [ ] **Parity issue.** The plan applied stricter review, visit limits, or day limits to
      behavioral health than to comparable medical/surgical care.
      → Invoke the **Mental Health Parity and Addiction Equity Act (MHPAEA)**. This is a
      powerful and underused argument. Demand the plan's medical necessity criteria and
      its comparative analysis.

## Plan type matters

- **Employer self-funded (ERISA):** appeal path is in your Summary Plan Description.
  You have a federal right to *all documents relied upon* free of charge, 29 C.F.R.
  § 2560.503-1. External review still available.
- **Fully insured / marketplace:** state insurance department regulates. External review
  is state-run and binding.
- **Medicaid/Medicare:** different, faster appeal tracks with hard deadlines. Ask for a
  fair hearing.

## After the internal appeal

1. **Second-level internal appeal** if the plan offers one — file it.
2. **External review.** Once internal appeals are exhausted (or the plan misses its own
   deadline), request independent external review. It is **free**, decided by
   physicians with no relationship to the plan, and **binding on the plan**. Request it
   through your state Department of Insurance, or via `externalappeal.cms.gov` for
   federally-administered review.
3. **Expedited review** is available if delay would jeopardize your health.
4. **Also file** with your state Department of Insurance consumer complaint division —
   and, if this is an employer plan, with the **U.S. Department of Labor, Employee
   Benefits Security Administration** (1-866-444-3272). EBSA will contact the plan on
   your behalf and this is often effective.
