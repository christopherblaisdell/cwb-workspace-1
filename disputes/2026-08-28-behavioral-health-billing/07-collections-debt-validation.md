# 07 — Debt Validation / Collections Response

> **⏰ HARD 30-DAY DEADLINE.** If a collection agency contacts you, you have **30 days
> from their first communication** to demand validation in writing. Send this
> immediately by Certified Mail w/ Return Receipt. Once sent, the collector **must
> cease collection activity** until it provides validation (15 U.S.C. § 1692g(b)).
>
> **Do not ignore a collections letter. Do not call them. Do not acknowledge the debt
> as valid. Do not make a "good faith" payment.**

---

## Key protections to know

- **FDCPA (15 U.S.C. § 1692g):** written dispute within 30 days → collection must stop
  until validation is provided.
- **Reg F (12 C.F.R. Part 1006):** limits contact frequency; you can demand they stop
  calling and communicate only in writing.
- **Medical debt credit reporting:** the three nationwide bureaus do not report paid
  medical collections, do not report medical collections under **$500**, and impose a
  **one-year delay** before any medical collection appears. Newer CFPB rules further
  restrict medical debt in credit decisions — check current status.
- **FCRA (15 U.S.C. § 1681s-2):** a furnisher must report a disputed debt as disputed.
- Never let a collector talk you into a payment plan before validation. Partial payment
  can restart the statute of limitations in some states.

## If it appears on your credit report

1. Dispute with **all three** bureaus (Equifax, Experian, TransUnion) in writing —
   certified mail, not the online form. Attach this letter and the delivery receipts.
   They have 30 days to investigate.
2. File a **CFPB complaint** at `consumerfinance.gov/complaint` naming both the
   collector and the facility. CFPB complaints get routed to the company with a
   response deadline and are unusually effective.
3. File with your **state Attorney General**.
4. If the collector continues collecting after a timely validation demand, or reports
   without noting the dispute, that is an FDCPA violation with statutory damages up to
   $1,000 plus attorney's fees. Consumer attorneys often take these on contingency at
   no cost to you.
