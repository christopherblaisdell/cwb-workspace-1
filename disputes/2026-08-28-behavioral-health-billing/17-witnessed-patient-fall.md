# 17 — Witnessed Patient Safety Event: Detox Patient's Fall on the Unsafe Stairway

**What you witnessed:** the facility has a rule that **detox patients may not go
upstairs**, because the stairway is old and **lacks a continuous handrail**. On a day the
facility was over census, they placed a **detox patient who was being given Ativan
(lorazepam)** upstairs anyway. You watched him fall down the stairs **head first, in a
somersault**, land unconscious, and make gurgling sounds. You were told he was
transported to a trauma center **by helicopter**.

---

## ⚠️ Your role here is different — read this first

**You are a witness, not a victim.** You have no personal damages from this event, and it
does not add money to your claim.

It does something else, and it may matter more: **it is the fact most likely to bring a
regulator through their door quickly.**

A licensing agency receiving "billing dispute plus religious discrimination" triages it
into a queue. A licensing agency receiving "**I personally witnessed a sedated detox
patient fall headfirst down a stairway the facility's own policy declares unsafe, because
they were over census, and he was flown out by trauma helicopter**" sends someone. Often
within days.

Report it because it should be reported. That it also blows your other complaints wide
open is a consequence, not the reason.

---

## ⭐ Why this is devastating for them

This isn't an accident. Based on your account, it's a **known hazard, a written policy
acknowledging that hazard, a deliberate deviation from that policy for business reasons,
and a foreseeable catastrophic result.** Each element makes the next one worse.

| Element | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **The rule exists** | The facility's own policy is a written admission that the stairway is dangerous and that detox patients are at elevated risk on it. They cannot claim they didn't know. |
| **No continuous handrail** | Independent building and life safety code problem. Handrails are required, not optional. |
| **The patient was on Ativan** | Lorazepam causes sedation, dizziness, and **ataxia** — impaired balance and coordination. Fall risk is a labeled, well-known effect. Giving it is appropriate in withdrawal management; placing that patient on an unsafe stairway is not. |
| **They were over census** | This is the killer. The deviation wasn't clinical judgment — it was **capacity**. They put a sedated patient in a known-dangerous location because they had too many patients. |
| **Catastrophic outcome** | Unconscious, gurgling (a sign of airway compromise), trauma center, air transport. |

The phrase a regulator will focus on: **they broke their own safety rule because they
were full.**

---

## Where this gets reported

### 1. ⭐⭐⭐ The Joint Commission — this is a sentinel event

A fall resulting in serious injury is a **sentinel event** under Joint Commission
terminology. TJC's patient safety event reporting exists precisely for this.

- File at `jointcommission.org` → **"Report a Patient Safety Event."**
- **This is your single highest-value report.** TJC accreditation is tied to
  reimbursement; a credible sentinel event report from an eyewitness triggers real
  scrutiny.
- Include: the policy prohibiting detox patients upstairs, the census pressure, the
  medication, the missing handrail, and the outcome.
- Then add your other issues — the cameras, the records, the discrimination. A surveyor
  already on site for a fall will see the monitors in the hallway.

### 2. ⭐⭐⭐ AHCA and DCF — adverse incident reporting

Florida licensed facilities have **mandatory adverse incident reporting** obligations.
Serious injuries and unexpected transfers to acute care generally must be reported to the
state within a defined window. *Verify the specific requirement for this facility's
license type — Fla. Stat. § 395.0197 for AHCA-licensed facilities, and DCF requirements
under Ch. 397 / Fla. Admin. Code r. 65D-30 for substance abuse providers.*

**Ask the agency directly whether an adverse incident report was filed for this event.**

- If they filed one → the state already has a record, and your account corroborates it.
- **If they didn't → that is an independent violation**, and it establishes a pattern of
  concealment that maps onto the missing HIPAA breach notification in
  [14](14-hipaa-breach.md).

Either answer helps. Ask the question.

- **AHCA:** `ahca.myflorida.com` → File a Complaint, or 1-888-419-3456
- **DCF SAMH:** Circuit 19 / Southeast Region (Martin County)

### 3. ⭐⭐ Fire Marshal / building code — the handrail

A stairway without a continuous handrail in a residential health care occupancy is a
**Life Safety Code (NFPA 101) and Florida Building Code** problem, independent of the
fall.

- **Martin County / City of Stuart Fire Marshal** — life safety inspection
- **Martin County Building Department** — code compliance
- AHCA also conducts life safety surveys

This is attractive because it's **objectively verifiable**. An inspector either finds a
continuous handrail or doesn't. No credibility contest, no chart to argue with.

### 4. Florida Attorney General

Goes to the pattern: a facility admitting patients beyond safe capacity while marketing
individualized, professional care.

## ⭐⭐⭐ Independent corroboration you can obtain yourself

**This is the most valuable part of this file.** Unlike everything else in your case, this
event generated records held by **government agencies** — not by Remedy, and not
dependent on your credibility.

Under Florida's public records law (**Fla. Stat. Ch. 119**), you can request:

- **EMS / fire rescue run report** for that address and date
- **911 call recording and CAD (computer-aided dispatch) log**
- **Trauma alert record** — air transport is logged
- **Fire marshal inspection history** for the property

These will be redacted for patient PHI — that's fine and expected. **You don't need the
patient's identity.** You need proof that on that date, at that address, EMS responded to
a fall and a trauma helicopter transported someone. That single fact, from a neutral
government source, corroborates your account of the event and — by extension — your
credibility on everything else in this file.

> **Why this matters so much:** a defense lawyer's first move is to suggest a former
> addiction treatment patient misremembers things. A county fire rescue run report is
> impervious to that argument.

## ⏰ The stairway may be on camera

Think about this: **they put cameras in every bedroom.** It is entirely plausible there
was a camera covering that stairway or the landing.

**The fall may exist on video.**

If it does, it is the most important piece of evidence in any case arising from this
event — and the facility has every incentive to let it overwrite. Typical retention on
these systems runs 30–90 days.

The preservation letter in [15-surveillance-cameras.md](15-surveillance-cameras.md)
already demands preservation of all footage during your dates of service, which covers
this. **Send it now.** Do not wait.

If you can pin the date of the fall, add a specific line item:

> "This demand expressly includes, without limitation, all video and audio recordings,
> metadata, and access logs from any device covering the stairway, stairwell, landings,
> and adjacent hallways on [DATE], and for the seven days before and after that date."

## ⚠️ Cautions

**1. Don't identify the other patient.**
If you know his name, **do not put it in a public complaint, a review, or a letter.**
Describe him by date, time, and circumstance. Regulators are authorized recipients and
can obtain identity through proper channels. You publishing it would be its own privacy
violation — and after everything in this file, you don't want to be on that side of it.

**2. Don't try to find him or his family.**
It would look like solicitation, could be characterized as interference, and it isn't
your place. If they surface on their own, that's different — and their claim would be far
larger than yours.

**3. Be precise about what you saw versus what you were told.**
- **Saw:** the fall, the landing, unconsciousness, the gurgling sounds.
- **Told:** the helicopter, the trauma center, the outcome.

Say so explicitly in your statement. "I was told" is not a weakness — mislabeling hearsay
as firsthand observation *is*, and it's the kind of thing that gets a witness's entire
account discounted.

**4. Pin down the date.**
Cross-reference against your program schedule, texts to your wife, or anything else
timestamped. The public records request is far easier with a date, though a range works.

**5. You have no claim from this.**
Unless witnessing it caused you documented psychological harm — which is possible and
worth mentioning to a clinician if true — this doesn't add to your damages. Its value is
regulatory pressure and corroboration.

- That you understood the facility to be over census that day, and how you knew
- That the patient was in detox and receiving Ativan, and **how you knew that** *(be
  careful and precise here — if you knew because he told you, say that)*
- **What you personally saw**, in sequence
- **What you were told afterward**, identified as such, and by whom
- Whether staff said anything to residents afterward
- Whether anyone asked you for a statement, or whether the facility documented that you
  were a witness
- Whether the stairway or its use changed after the incident

> That last point is worth attention. **If they installed a handrail or changed the rules
> after the fall**, that's a subsequent remedial measure — and it demonstrates they knew
> the hazard was real. Note anything you observed.
