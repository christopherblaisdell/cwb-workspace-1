# 10 — Incident Statement (Write This Today)

> **Why today:** a contemporaneous written account carries far more weight than a
> recollection produced months later, after you've spoken with lawyers and regulators.
> Every agency you file with will want this. Write it once, well, and reuse it.
>
> **Florida allows an unsworn declaration** under Fla. Stat. § 92.525 using the closing
> language below. It has the force of a sworn statement without a notary — though
> getting it notarized costs nothing at most banks and looks better.

---

## How to write it

**Do:**
- Write in first person, past tense, chronological order.
- Use quotation marks only for words you are confident are verbatim.
- Say "approximately" or "I believe" where you're unsure. Precision about your own
  uncertainty makes the rest of the statement more credible, not less.
- Describe what happened and what you observed. Facts, not conclusions.
- Note who else was present, even if you only know first names or descriptions.

**Don't:**
- Don't editorialize or argue. No "this was outrageous." Let the facts do it.
- Don't characterize motives — say what he did and said, not what he intended.
- Don't guess at dates. Write "on or about" and explain how you narrowed it.
- Don't include anything you aren't certain of. One overstated detail undermines
  everything else.

---

## Template

```
DECLARATION OF [YOUR FULL LEGAL NAME]

I, [YOUR FULL LEGAL NAME], residing at [YOUR ADDRESS], state the following of
my own personal knowledge:

1.  I was a resident at [FACILITY LEGAL NAME], located at [FACILITY ADDRESS],
    from on or about [ADMIT DATE] through on or about [DISCHARGE DATE].

2.  At the time of my admission I was in crisis and was seeking care for
    [general description — you need not detail your diagnosis].

    > ⚠️ **Do not write "I entered treatment voluntarily" until you have checked
    > the admission packet.** This statement is signed under penalty of perjury,
    > and the answer is currently unverified. Look for "involuntary examination,"
    > "certificate," "petition," a law enforcement transport form, or a 72-hour
    > hold reference. If any Baker Act or Marchman Act process touched the
    > admission at any point, a state actor is in the picture and the legal
    > analysis changes substantially. See [FACT-REGISTER.md](FACT-REGISTER.md)
    > § 5.10.

3.  I am a member of The Satanic Temple, which the Internal Revenue Service
    recognized as a church in 2019. I do not hold theistic religious beliefs.

BACKGROUND: PROGRAM CONTENT

4.  The programming at [FACILITY] consisted substantially of twelve-step
    material. [Describe what the daily schedule looked like: how many hours per
    day, what the sessions consisted of — Big Book readings, step work, prayer,
    references to God or a Higher Power. Estimate hours per day or week if you
    can.]

5.  [State whether any secular or evidence-based alternative was offered. If
    the facility offered nothing else, say so plainly.]

6.  On or about [DATE], I asked [NAME AND TITLE] whether a secular or
    cognitive-behavioral alternative was available. I was told, in substance:
    "[QUOTE OR PARAPHRASE — mark clearly which it is]." [State what, if
    anything, was offered as an accommodation. State whether anyone told you a
    grievance process existed.]

THE GROUP SESSION INCIDENT

7.  On or about [DATE — narrow it as best you can and explain how, e.g. "which
    I believe was during my second week, based on [reference point]"], I
    attended a group session at the facility. The group was called [NAME OF
    GROUP, if known] and was scheduled for approximately [TIME].

8.  The session was led by [FACILITATOR NAME], whose title I understood to be
    [TITLE / ROLE AS YOU KNEW IT]. Approximately [NUMBER] other residents were
    present.

9.  During the session, [FACILITATOR NAME] asked each participant in turn to
    state his or her religious beliefs.

10. When my turn came, I stated that I am an atheist.

11. [FACILITATOR NAME] then began a prayer with the other participants in the
    group. He placed his arms around the participants on either side of him and
    turned his back to me.

12. As he did so, he said words to the effect of: "SOME of us will be praying,"
    placing audible emphasis on the word "some."

13. I was not included in the prayer or in the group formed around it. [State
    what you did during that period: whether you remained seated, whether you
    left, whether anyone acknowledged you, how long it lasted.]

14. [State whether anyone — staff or resident — said anything to you about it
    afterward.]

15. [State whether you reported the incident, to whom, on what date, and what
    response you received. If you did not report it, you may state why — for
    example, that no grievance process was explained to you, or that you did
    not feel safe doing so as a resident dependent on the facility.]

16. To my knowledge, the other residents present in that session were: [list
    first names or physical descriptions — do NOT contact them]. Their identities
    should appear on the facility's group attendance roster for that date.

EFFECT

17. [Describe factually how this affected your treatment and your participation
    going forward. For example: whether you disengaged from groups, whether you
    felt unable to speak openly, whether you stopped raising concerns, whether
    it affected your willingness to remain in treatment. Keep it concrete and
    avoid dramatization — the facts are strong enough.]

18. I was billed by [FACILITY] for the treatment program of which this group
    session was a part. I have requested an itemized statement and my complete
    medical records in writing on [DATES], and as of the date of this statement
    the facility has [not responded / responded as follows: ___].

Under penalties of perjury, I declare that I have read the foregoing and that
the facts stated in it are true.


_______________________________          _______________
[YOUR FULL LEGAL NAME]                   Date
```

## After you write it

- [ ] Save as `evidence/[DATE]-incident-statement-SIGNED.pdf`
- [ ] Consider having it notarized — free at most banks and credit unions
- [ ] **Do not send it to the facility yet.** Hold it. It goes in the regulatory
      complaints and, if needed, to an attorney. Sending it early tells them exactly
      what you have before you've obtained the records that corroborate it.
- [ ] Write down the facilitator's name separately so we can check his licensure through
      Florida DOH (`flhealthsource.gov`) and the Florida Certification Board
- [ ] Keep narrowing the date — check texts to your wife, call logs, the program
      schedule, anything with a timestamp

## Supplemental statement from your wife

If you told her about the incident while you were still there, a short separate
declaration from her — "on or about [DATE], my husband told me by phone that…" — is
independent corroboration of both the event and its timing. Worth doing while she's
filling out the intake form. Same declaration format, same closing language.

Text messages from that period are even better. **Save them now**, before a phone gets
replaced or a backup rolls over.
