# 18 — Food Safety: Broken Refrigerator, Spoiled Food, and the 67°F Log

**What happened:** the refrigerator was not working and food was rotting. Residents
consistently drank **spoiled milk**. You reported it several times, **including in
writing**. The facility kept a **daily refrigerator temperature log** recording **67°F**
each day — and took no corrective action.

---

## ⭐ Why the log is the single best document in this entire case

Most of your case requires someone to believe your account over the facility's records.
**Not this one.** This is their own record, in their own handwriting, proving the
violation.

**67°F is not "a little warm." It is catastrophic.**

| Standard | Requirement |
|---|---|
| FDA Food Code / Florida food hygiene rules | Cold holding at **41°F or below** |
| The "temperature danger zone" | **41°F – 135°F** — where pathogenic bacteria multiply rapidly |
| What they logged | **67°F, every day** |

67°F is room temperature. It is **26 degrees above the legal maximum** and squarely in the
middle of the danger zone. Milk held at 67°F spoils within hours. Raw or cooked
refrigerated foods become dangerous quickly. This isn't a technicality — it is the exact
condition food safety rules exist to prevent.

### The pattern, for the third time

Look at what your case now establishes:

| Hazard | Their own documentation | Their action |
|---|---|---|
| The stairway | A **written policy** barring detox patients from it | Violated it for census; never fixed the handrail |
| The refrigerator | A **daily log reading 67°F** | Nothing |
| Your complaints | You reported it **in writing** | Nothing |

Three separate hazards. Three instances of the facility **creating a written record
demonstrating it knew**, and then doing nothing.

That is no longer "mistakes were made." It is a **documented institutional pattern of
recording known hazards and declining to act on them.** That framing is what moves a
regulator from issuing a citation to imposing systemic corrective action — and it's what
makes a jury angry.

**Use that framing explicitly in every complaint.**

### And it defeats the obvious defense

A facility's standard response to a food complaint is "we weren't aware." **They logged it
daily.** They cannot claim ignorance of a number they wrote down themselves, every day,
for the duration of your stay.

## ⭐ Your written complaint may be the most valuable document you have

You reported this **in writing**, while you were still a resident, before any dispute
existed.

That is **contemporaneous documentary evidence created by you, inside their system, with
no litigation motive**. It's worth more than anything you write today, because nobody can
argue you drafted it for a lawsuit.

It also creates a fork where both outcomes favor you:

- **If they produce it** → it proves actual notice, proves you complained, corroborates
  your account, and starts the clock on their inaction.
- **If they can't produce it** → they either destroyed a grievance record or never
  logged one, which is itself a licensure violation. Facilities are required to maintain
  grievance records.

Letter [#01](01-itemized-bill-and-records-request.md) now demands both your written
complaint and the grievance log.

**Do you have a copy?** Think about how you submitted it — a grievance form, an email, a
note handed to staff, the patient portal, a message your wife sent on your behalf. If any
copy exists on your side, that's better still.

## Who regulates this — a separate set of inspectors

Food service is a **different regulatory track** with **different inspectors**. That's
valuable: it's another independent agency with authority to walk in.

| Body | Jurisdiction | How to file |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Florida DOH — Martin County Health Department** | **Institutional food service inspection.** Residential facility food service is inspected under the state food hygiene rules (Fla. Admin. Code ch. 64E-11). *Verify jurisdiction for this license type.* This is your primary food complaint route. | Martin County Health Department, Environmental Health division |
| **AHCA** | Dietary services standards for licensed facilities | `ahca.myflorida.com`, 1-888-419-3456 |
| **DCF SAMH — Circuit 19** | Ch. 397 licensure; resident health, safety, and welfare | Southeast Region |
| **The Joint Commission** | Environment of care and nutritional/dietary services standards | `jointcommission.org` |

> "Has this facility been inspected for food service, and are there records of violations

Prior violations would establish a history. And **request the inspection history under
Chapter 119** along with the fire marshal records in
[17-witnessed-patient-fall.md](17-witnessed-patient-fall.md) — same request, add the

## ❓ What I need to know

**1. Did anyone get sick?** ⭐ This is the question that matters most.

- Did **you** experience nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, cramping, or fever?
- Did other residents?
- Did anyone report it to nursing? Was it documented?
- Was anyone sent out for medical evaluation?

> **Why this matters clinically and legally:** GI symptoms in a detox patient are
> genuinely dangerous — dehydration and electrolyte disturbance on top of withdrawal — and
> they are easily **misattributed to withdrawal itself**. If residents were treated for
> "withdrawal symptoms" that were actually foodborne illness, that is a serious clinical
> failure, and it may be visible in the medication administration records and nursing
> notes you've already requested.
>
> It's also the difference between a regulatory complaint and **personal damages**. If you
> got sick, you were injured.

**2. Your written complaint**
- How did you submit it, and roughly when?
- Do you have a copy or any record of it?
- What response, if any?
- Did you complain verbally as well — to whom?

**3. The log**
- How did you see it? Posted on the refrigerator, on a clipboard, in the kitchen?
- **Did it read exactly 67°F every single day?** *(An identical number daily is itself
  suspicious — it may indicate the log was filled in without actual measurement, which
  would be falsification of records.)*
- Who signed or initialed it?
- Roughly what date range did you observe?

**4. Scope**
- Was it one refrigerator or several? Walk-in or residential unit?
- Was the milk visibly or only tasted as spoiled? Curdled, sour, off-smelling?
- Was other food visibly rotting? What?
- Did staff eat the same food?
- Did staff ever acknowledge the problem?
- Was food ever thrown out or replaced?

**5. Other residents** — who else complained? First names only. **Do not contact them.**

## One more note on the identical daily reading

If the log genuinely recorded **67°F every single day**, consider what that implies. Real
refrigerator temperatures fluctuate — door openings, restocking, ambient conditions, time
of day. A perfectly constant reading across weeks suggests one of two things:

1. Nobody actually measured; someone copied the same number forward. That is
   **falsification of a required record.**
2. They measured accurately and the unit was simply dead — running at ambient room
   temperature continuously.

Both are serious. The first is a records integrity violation that connects directly to the
forged signatures in [12-forged-signatures.md](12-forged-signatures.md) — a facility that
signs patients' names is a facility that might also fill in logs it never took.

**Mention this observation to regulators.** Inspectors know exactly what a suspiciously
constant log means, and it's the sort of detail that expands a narrow complaint into a
full records audit.
