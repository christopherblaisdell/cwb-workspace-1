# 12 — Forged Signatures on Intake Paperwork

> **This is now the most serious issue in the case, and it is bigger than $4,000.**
>
> You state you never signed any of the intake paperwork — that facility staff signed
> your name for you.

---

## ⛔ Read this before you do anything else

**Do not tell the facility you know the signatures are forged. Not yet.**

Letter [#01](01-itemized-bill-and-records-request.md) already requests, at item 11:

> "Copies of every financial responsibility agreement, admission consent, or payment
> authorization **bearing my signature** that your office relies upon to assert this
> debt."

That request is neutral on its face. It reads like routine documentation gathering.

**Send it exactly as written.** If they produce documents with a forged signature while
unaware that you dispute it, you have them in a fixed position. If you announce the
forgery first, you invite the response of "we'll look into that" — followed by documents
that are never located, or a sudden discovery of an unsigned file.

Get the documents first. Then confront.

---

## Why this changes the case

### 1. It may eliminate the debt entirely

The facility's claim to $4,000 rests on a contract — a financial responsibility
agreement in which you assumed liability for cost-sharing. **If your signature on that
agreement is forged, there is no agreement.** A forged signature does not bind you.

This is a complete defense to the debt, not a discount argument. Everything else in this
case file goes to reducing the amount. This goes to whether any of it is owed.

### 2. Forgery is a crime in Florida

- **Fla. Stat. § 831.01** — Forgery
- **Fla. Stat. § 831.02** — Uttering a forged instrument (using it)

Both are felonies. *Verify current text and grading.* I am not suggesting you file a
criminal complaint today — but a facility's compliance officer, its accreditor, and its
insurers all understand exactly what these words mean, and that changes how a letter is
received.

### 3. Falsified medical records are a licensure violation

Consent forms and intake documents are part of the medical record. Staff signing a
patient's name into the chart is falsification of records — actionable by DCF, AHCA, the
Joint Commission, and (as to any licensed individual involved) the Florida Department of
Health.

### 4. Forged consent means treatment without informed consent

If the consent-to-treatment form was signed by staff, you did not give documented
informed consent. In a residential behavioral health setting, that is a serious patient
rights violation — and Florida's patient rights statutes (Fla. Stat. § 397.501 /
§ 394.459) address consent directly.

### 5. If forged documents were used to obtain payment, that is fraud

- Insurance claims supported by forged consent or forged financial agreements →
  **Fla. Stat. § 817.234**, insurance fraud.
- Forged HIPAA authorization → improper disclosure of your protected health information,
  independently reportable to HHS OCR.
- **If Medicaid or Medicare paid any portion** → federal **False Claims Act** exposure
  and the **Florida Medicaid Fraud Control Unit**. Verify whether either paid anything.

### 6. It reframes everything else

A facility willing to sign a patient's name is a facility whose documentation cannot be
taken at face value. That matters when you audit the itemized bill against the chart —
progress notes, group attendance rosters, and treatment plans all become suspect. It
also means: **expect signatures on documents you never saw.**

## What to do, in order

### Step 1 — Preserve what you know now

- [ ] Add a section to your incident statement
      ([10-incident-statement.md](10-incident-statement.md)) covering the paperwork.
      Include:
      - What you actually remember about intake — who you spoke with, where you were,
        your condition at the time
      - Whether you were physically capable of signing (intoxicated, in withdrawal,
        sedated, medicated?)
      - Whether any document was ever placed in front of you
      - Whether you were given copies of anything
      - How you learned the signatures were not yours
- [ ] **Collect authentic signature exemplars.** Ten to fifteen genuine signatures from
      around that time period — driver's license, bank signature card, checks, credit
      card receipts, leases, prior medical forms, tax returns. A questioned-documents
      examiner needs comparison samples. Scan them to `evidence/signature-exemplars/`.

### Step 2 — Send letter #01 unchanged

Do not modify it. Item 11 already gets you the documents.

### Step 3 — When the documents arrive

- [ ] Compare each signature against your exemplars.
- [ ] Note which documents bear a signature and which don't.
- [ ] Look at the **dates and times** — were multiple documents signed at a moment when
      you were in detox, sedated, or not yet on the premises?
- [ ] Check whether the **same handwriting** appears across documents that should have
      been signed on different days.
- [ ] Check for a **witness or staff signature** on the same forms — that identifies who
      was present.
- [ ] Do **not** write on the originals. Work from copies.

### Step 4 — Then escalate

Once you hold the documents, the forgery allegation goes into:
- Letter [#04](04-formal-billing-dispute.md), as a defense to the debt
- The DCF / AHCA licensure complaint
- The Joint Commission complaint
- The FL DOH complaint against any licensed individual who signed
- The insurer's Special Investigations Unit
- The Florida Attorney General

## ⚠️ Cautions

- **Do not accuse anyone by name of a crime in writing** until you have the documents and
  ideally counsel. Say: "the signature appearing on this document is not mine and I did
  not sign it." That is a factual statement you can stand behind. Let the regulators and
  the lawyers characterize it.
- **Do not sign anything the facility sends you now**, including any "corrected"
  paperwork, settlement, or release, without counsel reviewing it.
- If they suddenly offer to write off the balance in exchange for a release — that is
  information about how they view their exposure. Don't sign it without advice.
- **Preserve everything.** Once litigation is reasonably anticipated, they have a duty to
  preserve records. A written notice to preserve (a litigation hold letter) may be worth
  sending — ask counsel.
