# 🟠 TOMORROW — free, mostly online, about 2 hours

## 4. Report the stairway as an ongoing hazard

**Lead with this sentence:**

> "To my knowledge the stairway still has no continuous handrail and detox patients may
> still be housed upstairs. I believe patients in that building are at risk today."

Ongoing danger gets someone dispatched. A historical complaint gets filed.

- **Martin County / City of Stuart Fire Marshal** — life safety and handrail code
- **Martin County Building Department** — code compliance

## 5. File the Joint Commission report — your highest-value single filing

`jointcommission.org` → **"Report a Patient Safety Event"**

A fall causing serious injury is a **sentinel event**. Their accreditation is tied to
getting paid, so this gets real attention.

Include, in this order: the fall, the policy they broke, the census pressure, the missing
handrail that was never fixed, the bedroom cameras with public monitors, the records sent
to a law office, and the group session incident.

## 6. File AHCA and DCF complaints

- **AHCA** — `ahca.myflorida.com` → File a Complaint, or 1-888-419-3456
- **DCF Substance Abuse & Mental Health** — Circuit 19 / Southeast Region (Martin County)

**Request an unannounced inspection.** And ask one specific question:

> "Was an adverse incident report filed for the patient fall on or about [DATE]?"

If yes, the state already has a record. If no, that's an independent violation. Either
answer helps you.

## 7. Call Disability Rights Florida — **1-800-342-0823**

Free. Federally funded. Patient rights in behavioral health facilities is literally their
mandate, and they can inspect facilities.

**Make this call.** It costs nothing and they may take the whole thing.

## 7a. File the food safety complaint — **Martin County Health Department**

Environmental Health division. A separate agency with its own inspectors and its own
authority to walk in.

Tell them: **the facility's own daily log recorded 67°F**, residents were served spoiled
milk and rotting food, and you complained in writing with no response. Safe cold holding
is 41°F or below — 67°F is 26 degrees over, squarely in the bacterial danger zone.

Ask them: *"Has this facility been inspected for food service, and are there prior
violations relating to refrigeration?"* See
[18-food-safety-refrigeration.md](18-food-safety-refrigeration.md).
